Green Bank for Rural America

Appalachian Community Capital established the Green Bank to help local lenders bring clean energy investment, lower energy costs, and new jobs to Appalachia, energy communities, and underserved rural areas.

Explore Financing Priorities Program Resources

Program Overview

The Green Bank is an ACC subsidiary serving rural communities, including places affected by coal and power plant closures. Its model pairs capital for community lenders with technical assistance for lenders, project developers, and local partners.

The original plan prioritized 582 counties, including communities in the 13-state Appalachian region, energy communities, and underserved rural and Native communities nationwide. View the service area and eligibility map.

In 2024, ACC announced a $500 million award through the EPA's Clean Communities Investment Accelerator. For information about the grant and program activity, see the Green Bank's leadership statement.

Financing Priorities

These are the types of projects the program was designed to finance through community lenders.

Clean Energy Businesses

Financing for businesses such as solar installers, battery manufacturers, and energy efficiency service providers.

Energy-Efficient Buildings

Energy upgrades and renewable power for businesses, schools, health facilities, and affordable housing.

Local Power and Storage

Community and rooftop solar, battery storage, and other distributed generation such as wind and geothermal.

Clean Transportation

Electric fleets and school buses, charging infrastructure, and shared transportation suited to rural communities.

Lender and Community Support

Capital for Community Lenders

The original plan called for $400 million in lending capital for about 100 community lenders and investors, including community development financial institutions (CDFIs) and public or nonprofit loan funds.

Technical Assistance

Planned support included financial modeling, project design, grant preparation, and help structuring financing with tools such as bridge loans, loan guarantees, and tax credits.

Community and Workforce Partnerships

Community organizations help identify local priorities. Workforce boards, colleges, unions, and training providers help prepare residents for jobs in the energy sector.
